# Hop — Motion Video Story

## Title
**A better way to get there**

## Format
Product-story motion video for Hop/Ridly, the Denmark-focused carpooling app. Target length: about 2 minutes 30 seconds. The app is available on Android and iOS; show the same journey on either device.

## Story

### 1. The day begins — 0:00–0:12

**Visual:** A quiet Danish morning. A map-like line draws between two cities; one car travels the route with empty seats. Cut between a passenger checking the time and a driver looking at the cost of an otherwise solo drive. Their screens light up with the Hop logo.

**Voiceover:** “Every day, people are heading the same way. Hop helps them make the journey together.”

**Motion / sound:** Soft road ambience becomes a warm, rhythmic music bed. The route line curves into the Hop mark.

### 2. A simple start — 0:12–0:27

**Visual:** The animated splash resolves into onboarding. Quick, clean screen transitions show the sign-up form, email verification, and the confirmation screen. A second user signs in; a brief alternate beat shows “Forgot password” leading to a reset email and a new password.

**Voiceover:** “Create an account, verify your email, and you’re ready to ride—or offer a seat.”

**On-screen text:** “One account. Two ways to travel.”

### 3. Find a ride — 0:27–0:48

**Visual:** Nia opens Passenger Home. Saved Home and Work places and a recent search appear as shortcuts. She enters a route, picks a date and number of seats, then taps Search. Search Results reveal matching trips. She opens one: driver profile and rating, pickup-to-destination route, departure details, available seats, price, and—on a threshold trip—progress toward the minimum.

**Voiceover:** “Choose where you’re going, when you’re leaving, and how many seats you need. Compare trips, meet your driver, and see the details before you book.”

**Motion / sound:** Search fields connect with a tracing route; matching trip cards rise into view. A subtle pulse highlights the selected trip.

### 4. Reserve the seat — 0:48–1:03

**Visual:** Nia reviews the booking and confirms. A success state appears. In My Trips, her upcoming ride opens to active trip details, with a direct chat entry. A small branch shows the cancellation confirmation before departure, then returns to the trip list.

**Voiceover:** “Confirm your booking, keep track of it in My Trips, and stay in touch with your driver. Plans change? Your booking tools are right there.”

**On-screen text:** “Your trip, all in one place.”

### 5. Make room for another passenger — 1:03–1:27

**Visual:** Switch to Malik’s Driver Home: trip cards, earnings and goals, demand insights, tips, and a repost option for a previous route. He selects “Post a trip.” A quick split-screen presents two choices:

- **Recurring commute (Model A):** enter the route, recurring weekdays, departure time, and seats; review the suggested price; publish.
- **One-off trip (Model B):** enter a single route and departure, choose seats and a minimum passenger threshold, and review the price and deadline.

Show two brief outcomes for the one-off option: the minimum is met and the trip is confirmed; in an alternate outcome, the deadline passes without enough passengers and the trip is cancelled, with affected passengers notified.

**Voiceover:** “Drivers can share a regular commute or post a one-off journey with a clear passenger minimum. Review the suggested price, publish, and manage every trip from one place.”

**Motion / sound:** The two trip models fan out from a single “Post a trip” button. Seats fill one by one; the threshold indicator resolves into either a confirmation or a cancellation notice.

### 6. Before, during, and after the ride — 1:27–1:49

**Visual:** Malik’s My Trips shows an upcoming ride; he edits its details, then opens the active trip to see passengers. Nia and Malik exchange a short in-app chat. Notifications slide in for a booking update and new message. The journey reaches its destination. Malik marks the trip complete; both users leave a star rating for the other.

**Voiceover:** “From trip updates and real-time chat to ratings after arrival, the important details stay connected to the ride.”

**Motion / sound:** Chat bubbles travel along the route line. At arrival, the line settles; two star ratings animate into place.

### 7. Settle directly — 1:49–2:07

**Visual:** Nia sees the fare and Malik’s MobilePay number, opens MobilePay, and marks that she sent the payment. Malik sees her status and confirms receipt. A quick alternate beat shows a payment dispute option. The film makes clear the payment leaves the app and goes directly between the two people.

**Voiceover:** “After the ride, the passenger pays the driver directly through MobilePay. The app keeps both sides up to date.”

**On-screen text:** “Direct person-to-person payment.”

### 8. The rest of the journey — 2:07–2:23

**Visual:** A concise montage: Malik checks his monthly tax dashboard and opens the annual report download; Nia updates her profile and MobilePay details; a notification is marked read; Help Centre search opens a how-it-works answer; Settings reveals notification controls, Privacy Policy, Terms, and log out. A profile card for another user shows their public ride identity.

**Voiceover:** “Profiles, trip history, notifications, help, and driver tax summaries make Hop useful beyond the ride itself.”

**Motion / sound:** Each screen appears as a quick, legible card—no tiny UI text—then folds back into the route.

### 9. End card — 2:23–2:30

**Visual:** The passenger and driver continue in the same car, now sharing the road. The route line completes a gentle Hop-shaped loop. Logo and app-store devices appear.

**Voiceover:** “Hop. A better way to get there—together.”

**On-screen text:** “Find your ride. Share your route.”

## Coverage checklist

- **Getting started:** onboarding, sign-up, email verification, login, forgot-password and password reset.
- **Passenger:** home, saved places, recent searches, route/date/seat search, results, trip and driver details, booking confirmation and success, My Trips, active trip, chat, cancellation, rating, and settlement.
- **Driver:** role switch and driver setup (car details and MobilePay number), driver home, trip creation for recurring Model A and threshold-based Model B, price review, trip edit and trip management, completion, passenger ratings, tax dashboard, and annual report.
- **Shared:** notifications, chat list and conversation, own and other profiles, settings, Help Centre, privacy policy, and terms.
- **Lifecycle alternatives:** threshold met / not met; passenger paid / driver confirmed / payment dispute; booking kept / cancelled.
- **Platform and service context:** frame the same product on Android and iOS; use map-like motion as a visual metaphor, not a promise of a particular in-app map experience.

## Accuracy notes for production

- MobilePay settlement is **manual, direct person-to-person payment**: the passenger opens MobilePay, marks payment sent, and the driver confirms receipt. Do not portray Hop as collecting, holding, or automatically verifying money.
- Model B is a **one-off trip with a minimum-passenger threshold and deadline**; if the minimum is not met, the trip is cancelled and affected users are notified.
- Keep any route/map graphics illustrative; the client map experience is not a settled product decision.
- Admin operations are internal backend capabilities, not consumer-facing app screens. Do not show an admin dashboard as part of the customer journey.
